  • Visibility

    Google Business Profile: get verified, then let Gemini help

    Claim, verify, and optimise your listing so customers see your hours, photos, and contact info first.

    Show the steps

    Verification now takes planning, and it is where most new profiles get stuck. Work through these in order.

    Before you start

    1. Make your details match everywhere. Use your trading name, a +27 phone number and your address exactly as on your door, website and social pages.
    2. Keep the profile's phone number answered. Google may contact it by call, text or WhatsApp during verification. Tell whoever answers, because replies you give can appear on your profile.

    Verify

    1. Claim or add the business on Google Maps and choose “Verify”. Google picks the method. Video is now the most common. Phone or email appear for some older profiles. Postcards are rare.
    2. If it is a video, record it at the business. Aim for 1 to 2 minutes and show the signage, the location, your equipment or stock, and proof that you manage the business. Avoid showing faces or sensitive documents. Watch it before you send it.
    3. Allow up to 5 business days. If Google asks you to verify again after you change your name, address, category or hours, complete it. If no method works, contact Google support with photos of your signage, a recent utility bill and your website address.

    After you are verified

    1. If Gemini is available for your account, connect the profile to it. It can help update your hours, contacts and photos, draft replies to reviews, and report on impressions, website clicks and direction requests. Read every reply before it goes live.

  • Visibility

    How to rank in “near me” searches

    Google uses your address, reviews, and website content to match local buyers. Consistency wins.

    Show the steps

    Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot pay for a better ranking, but you control more of it than you think.

    1. Get verified. A verified profile is more likely to appear in search results. See the Google Business Profile guide on this page.
    2. Fill in every detail. Use your full address if customers visit, regular and special opening hours, and attributes such as parking or Wi-Fi. Wrong details can stop you appearing for the right searches.
    3. Choose the category that best describes what you sell. It is how Google matches you to what people search for.
    4. Add photos and videos of what you offer, so people can see it before they visit.
    5. Ask happy customers for reviews, and reply to every review. Reviews, ratings and links from other websites all add to how well known you are.
    6. Check your address is exactly right. Distance is the one factor you cannot change, because Google measures it from where the customer is to where you are.

  • Privacy

    Data collection & consent basics

    Tell people why you need their details, how you’ll use them, and give them an easy way to opt out.

    Show the steps

    A short guide to collecting customer details the POPIA way. It is general information, not legal advice.

    1. Ask only for what you need. Decide why you need each detail before you ask for it.
    2. Say why, in plain words. Tell people why you are collecting their details, how you will use them and who you will share them with. “We may use your data for anything” is not good enough.
    3. Where you need consent, make it a real choice. It must be voluntary, specific and informed. No pre-ticked boxes, silence is not consent, and do not bury it in terms people cannot refuse.
    4. Know when you do not need consent. For an order, an invoice or a contract, another lawful reason usually applies. Consent matters most for optional extras such as newsletters and marketing.
    5. Marketing by email, SMS or other electronic message needs prior consent, unless the person is an existing customer under the Act’s conditions. Every message should say who sent it and give an easy way to opt out. Stop when someone objects.
    6. Keep a record. Note when and how each person agreed, and any time they withdrew. The burden of proof is on you.

  • Privacy

    Identity consistency checklist

    Ensure your business name, phone, and address match exactly everywhere online. Mismatched data hurts rankings.

    Show the steps

    Google trusts details that match everywhere, and so do customers. Check these in order.

    1. Write your master copy once. Your trading name, your address and your phone number in +27 format, exactly as they should appear.
    2. Check the main places. Your Google Business Profile, your website (contact page and footer), Facebook and other social pages, review sites and business directories.
    3. Fix the mistakes that change the meaning. A wrong or old phone number, an old address, or a different business name. Small formatting differences, such as “St” and “Street”, are generally fine.
    4. Start with the most visible listings. Errors are quick to create and slow to fix, so fix what customers see first.
    5. Check again after any change. A move, a new number or a new name means going through the list once more.

  • Safety

    Spotting fake service providers

    Red flags: upfront full payments, vague contracts, pressure tactics. Always verify credentials before sharing access.

    Show the steps

    Before you pay anyone to build or manage your online presence, run through these checks.

    1. Check they are registered. Find their registration number and look it up on the CIPC company search.
    2. Check the address. Find it on a map. Be careful if you cannot find it or it does not look like a real business.
    3. Find their contact details somewhere other than their own website, such as a directory, a social page or a Google listing. Search for reviews and other people’s experiences before you pay.
    4. Be careful of prices far below everyone else’s, and of pressure to pay today. If it seems too good to be true, it probably is.
    5. Look closely at how they want to be paid. If they insist on EFT only, you have no way to dispute or reverse the payment.
    6. Get the price and what you receive in writing, make sure your domain and accounts are registered in your name, and never share passwords until you have checked who you are dealing with.

  • Safety

    Verify you own your domain

    Domains expire. Keep login credentials secure, enable auto-renew, and assign a backup contact to avoid losing your site.

    Show the steps

    If your web designer registered your domain, they may own it, not you. Check these in order.

    1. Find out who the registered owner is. Look your domain up at your registrar. The owner should be you or your business, not your web designer.
    2. Log in to the registrar account yourself. If you cannot, you do not control the domain. Use an email address you control.
    3. Turn on auto-renew, with a payment method that will not expire. Renewal reminders should go to an email you read. A .co.za domain can be renewed for up to five years.
    4. Keep the transfer lock on. It blocks unauthorised transfers. A .co.za domain has no authorisation code, only lock and unlock, so the lock is your main protection.
    5. Name a backup contact, a second email address or a person you trust, so renewals and access do not depend on one phone or one person.
    6. Write down your registrar, the login email and the renewal date, and keep the note somewhere safe.
