Know Your Rights

What Is Intimate Image Abuse?

IIA is the non-consensual distribution of intimate images. In South Africa, it is a criminal offence under the Cybercrimes Act.

Criminal Law

Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020

Section 16 makes it a criminal offence to unlawfully and intentionally distribute intimate images without consent. Penalties include fines and imprisonment of up to 3 years.

Key: Consent must be explicit, specific, and freely given.
Civil Remedy

Protection of Personal Information Act

POPIA provides civil remedies for the unlawful processing of personal information, including intimate images. You can claim damages for emotional distress and reputational harm.

Key: Information Regulator can issue enforcement notices.
Platform Action

Digital Platform Takedowns

Major platforms (Meta, Google, X/Twitter) have dedicated reporting channels for non-consensual intimate imagery. Reports are typically processed within 24-48 hours.

Key: Document everything before reporting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Intimate Image Abuse (IIA) includes any of the following without explicit consent:
  • Distributing, publishing, or sharing intimate images or videos of another person
  • Threatening to distribute intimate images (even if never actually shared)
  • Creating or distributing deepfake intimate imagery
  • Recording intimate moments without the subject's knowledge or consent
"Intimate" means showing nudity, sexual activity, or content a reasonable person would consider private.

The more evidence you can preserve, the stronger your case:
  • Screenshots of the images/videos with visible URLs and timestamps
  • Screenshots of any threatening messages or communications
  • Witness statements from anyone who saw the content
  • Records of platform reports and responses
  • Any admission or confession from the perpetrator
Store all evidence on a device the perpetrator cannot access. Consider using a trusted friend's device or cloud storage with 2FA.
Under the Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020, the penalties for IIA are:
  • First offence: Fine or imprisonment of up to 3 years, or both
  • Subsequent offences: Fine or imprisonment of up to 5 years, or both
Case timelines vary significantly depending on court backlogs, but the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) has prioritised GBV-related cases. Many cases are resolved within 12-24 months.
Action Plan

Your Step-by-Step Guide

Follow these steps to protect yourself, preserve evidence, and seek justice.

1. Secure Your Digital Space

Change all passwords, enable 2FA, and check for unauthorised access to accounts. Log out of all devices.

2. Document Everything

Screenshots with timestamps, URLs, and any communications. Store securely offline if possible.

3. Report to Platforms

Use in-app reporting for Meta, Google, X, TikTok. Most have dedicated IIA reporting channels.

4. File a Police Report

Visit your nearest SAPS station. Reference Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020, Section 16. Request a case number.

5. Seek Legal & Emotional Support

Contact Legal Aid, a private attorney, or the GBV Command Centre. You don't have to do this alone.

Critical

Do Not Delete Evidence

Even if it's painful to see, preserving the original posts and messages is essential for prosecution. Screenshot everything before reporting.

Important

You Cannot Be Prosecuted

Victims cannot be charged for possessing their own intimate images. You are the victim, not a criminal. The law protects you.

Remember

Shame Belongs to the Perpetrator

IIA is about power and control, not your actions. The shame is not yours to carry. Support is available, judgment-free.
