Data collection and consent basics

Tell people why you need their details, how you will use them, and give them an easy way to opt out. This is general information, not legal advice.

Why it matters

Privacy

Why it matters

Any business that collects names, phone numbers or email addresses is handling personal information, and the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) applies to it.

Customers also notice. A clear, honest explanation of why you need their details builds trust before the first order.

Steps

Privacy

What to do

Six steps

  1. Ask only for what you need. Decide why you need each detail before you ask for it.
  2. Say why, in plain words. Tell people why you are collecting their details, how you will use them and who you will share them with. “We may use your data for anything” is not good enough.
  3. Where you need consent, make it a real choice. It must be voluntary, specific and informed. No pre-ticked boxes, silence is not consent, and do not bury it in terms people cannot refuse.
  4. Know when you do not need consent. For an order, an invoice or a contract, another lawful reason usually applies. Consent matters most for optional extras such as newsletters and marketing.
  5. Marketing by email, SMS or other electronic message needs prior consent, unless the person is an existing customer under the Act’s conditions. Every message should say who sent it and give an easy way to opt out. Stop when someone objects.
  6. Keep a record. Note when and how each person agreed, and any time they withdrew. The burden of proof is on you.
Avoid

Privacy

Common mistakes

  • Pre-ticking a marketing box on a form.
  • Adding customers to a newsletter because they placed an order.
  • Collecting more details than the order needs, such as an ID number for a simple enquiry.
  • Having no record of when someone agreed, so you cannot show consent if asked.
Check

Privacy

Quick check

You are done when:

  • Every form or chat that collects details says why you want them.
  • Marketing consent is a separate, unticked choice.
  • You can find out when and how a person agreed.
